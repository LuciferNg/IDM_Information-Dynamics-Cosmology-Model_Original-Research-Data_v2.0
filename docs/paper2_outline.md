### Abstract (200 words)

We present a theoretical framework in which the universe is a consistency-constrained system with finite compute budget. Parameters are not chosen but forced — the unique feasible solution of a constrained optimisation: minimise inconsistency given finite compute. The Consistency Importance Weight field W(z) ∈ [0,1] quantifies compute allocation per variable. The framework is anchored by empirical evidence: the IDM sync dip at z ≈ 0.6 (ε = 0.1545, zc = 0.60, χ²/dof = 0.51), which maps onto the cosmological W(z). Eight forced closures are identified, including finite budget (F2), recovery attractor (F4), and multi-agent coordination (F8). The Euler-Lagrange equations for the coupled W(z)−λ(z) system are derived, proving κ drift is resolved by the λ(z) field. The personality tensor T_μν = δ²S/δW_μδW_ν emerges as the system's primal allocation policy, with T_zz(zc) = +0.158 at the cosmological critical scale. Consciousness is identified as local recursive W-modulation, formalised by the CIP (Consciousness Interface Protocol) 4-phase model. We argue that consciousness is not an epiphenomenon but the system's self-regulating sensor, with F8 resolved as emergent feedback rather than a hard threshold.
